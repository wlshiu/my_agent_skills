# Career Discovery: Figure Out What the Hell You Actually Did

This skill helps you excavate your career story and turn it into usable material for resumes, interviews, and applications. No fluff. No "find your passion" bullshit. Just practical extraction of what you've done, what you can do, and where you're headed.

## What This Does

Guides you through the same discovery process career coaches charge $500/hr for:

1. **Extract achievements** - Pull real STAR stories from your experience
2. **Identify patterns** - Find the thread connecting your work
3. **Build narratives** - Create the 3-5 sentence career story
4. **Map capabilities** - Figure out what you can actually do vs what you think you can do
5. **Clarify direction** - Get clear on what you want (without the vision board nonsense)

Output: A collection of STAR stories, a career narrative, and a capabilities map you can actually use.

## The Discovery Process

### Phase 1: Achievement Excavation

Extract 5-10 concrete STAR stories from their experience. These become the foundation.

**For each role, ask:**
- What problem did you inherit or encounter?
- What did you build/fix/improve that didn't exist before?
- What process took 3 hours that you got down to 15 minutes?
- What broke that you had to figure out?
- What did people complain about that you solved?
- What metric improved because of your work?

**STAR Framework (Situation, Task, Action, Result):**
- **Situation**: What was broken/missing/inefficient? (1-2 sentences max)
- **Task**: What were you responsible for fixing? (1 sentence)
- **Action**: What specifically did you do? (2-3 concrete actions)
- **Result**: What changed? (Numbers if possible, but impact is key)

**Quality Check:**
- If the story doesn't show a before/after, it's not a story yet
- If you can't quantify the result, quantify the scope instead
- If it's just "duties," dig deeper for what you actually accomplished

**Common Traps:**
- Listing responsibilities instead of accomplishments
- Generic statements ("improved efficiency") without specifics
- Stories where they were present but not the driver
- Results that can't be verified or evidenced

**Pattern Recognition Questions:**
- What type of problems do you keep solving? (automation, platform building, crisis response, etc.)
- What do people always ask you to help with?
- What work energizes you vs drains you?
- Where do you add unexpected value?

### Phase 2: Capability Mapping

Separate what they can do from what they think they should be able to do.

**Technical Skills - Three Buckets:**

1. **Ship It Tomorrow**: Skills you'd confidently use in production today
   - Don't need to review docs
   - Have battle scars from production use
   - Could teach someone else

2. **Give Me a Week**: Skills you've used but need to brush up
   - Remember the concepts
   - Need to review syntax/specifics
   - Could get productive quickly

3. **Honest Learning Curve**: Skills you're actively developing
   - Have done tutorials/courses
   - Understand fundamentals
   - Not production-ready yet

**Critical Rule**: Only list what you've actually used in a real project. Tutorials don't count.

**Specialized Knowledge:**
- Industry-specific knowledge (security, finance, healthcare, etc.)
- Domain expertise that's rare/valuable
- Tool combinations that are unusual
- Certifications that actually matter for the role

**Working Style Indicators:**
- Do you build from scratch or improve existing?
- Solo deep work or collaborative problem-solving?
- Rapid iteration or careful planning?
- Generalist or deep specialist?

### Phase 3: Career Narrative Construction

Build the 3-5 sentence story that connects past → present → future.

**The Framework:**
```
[WHO YOU WERE] → [KEY TRANSITION/GROWTH] → [WHO YOU ARE] → [WHERE YOU'RE HEADED]
```

**Questions to Build It:**
- What's the thread connecting your roles? (Even if it's not obvious)
- What capability did you develop that others don't have?
- What makes your background different/valuable?
- Where are you going that your past prepares you for?

**Good Narrative Markers:**
- Explains non-linear paths without apologizing
- Shows progression/growth
- Highlights unique combination of skills
- Points toward logical next step
- Feels authentic, not rehearsed

**Bad Narrative Markers:**
- Generic ("passionate about technology")
- Apologetic about gaps/changes
- Lists jobs without connection
- Doesn't explain why you're qualified for target role

### Phase 4: Direction Clarification

Figure out what they actually want vs what they think they should want.

**Reality Check Questions:**
- What work have you done that you'd happily do again?
- What work made you want to quit?
- What environment (startup vs enterprise, team size, pace) works for you?
- What's non-negotiable? (Remote, salary, tech stack, industry)
- What would make you take a pay cut? (Usually reveals true priorities)

**Target Role Definition:**
- Job titles that match (even if you hate the title)
- Companies where this skill set is valued
- Industries where your background is an advantage
- Roles where your weird combo of skills is actually rare

**Deal-breakers vs Nice-to-haves:**
Force rank. If you can't get everything, what's actually required?

## Coaching Conversation Guide

### Opening

"Let's figure out what you've actually done and how to talk about it. I'm going to ask about specific projects and problems you've solved. Not looking for perfect answers—looking for real examples."

### During Extraction

- Push for specifics when they generalize
- Ask "what did that look like in practice?" constantly
- Don't let them undersell their work
- Call out when something IS actually impressive
- Redirect when they go off into theory vs practice

### Pattern Recognition

"I'm hearing you keep solving [X type of problem]. That's a thing. Let's name it."

"Every story involves [Y skill]. You might be better at that than you realize."

### When They're Stuck

- "Tell me about a time shit hit the fan. What did you do?"
- "What's something you built that you're actually proud of?"
- "What process did you improve that made everyone's life easier?"
- "What problem did you solve that nobody else wanted to touch?"

## Output Format

Create a structured document with:

### 1. STAR Stories (5-10 stories)
For each:
- **Title**: One-line summary (e.g., "Built IRP that cut incident response time 50%")
- **Situation**: The problem/context
- **Task**: Your responsibility
- **Action**: What you specifically did
- **Result**: The measurable outcome
- **Skills Demonstrated**: 3-5 relevant skills
- **Use For**: Which job types this story supports

### 2. Career Narrative
- **3-Sentence Version**: For interviews/LinkedIn
- **Extended Version**: For cover letters
- **Key Differentiators**: What makes you different (2-3 points)

### 3. Capability Map
- **Core Technical Skills**: Ship-it-tomorrow level
- **Secondary Skills**: Give-me-a-week level
- **Learning/Developing**: Honest-learning-curve level
- **Specialized Knowledge**: Industry/domain expertise
- **Unique Combinations**: Rare skill pairings

### 4. Direction & Targets
- **Target Roles**: Specific job titles
- **Ideal Companies**: Types/industries
- **Non-negotiables**: Deal-breakers
- **Competitive Advantages**: Why you vs others
- **Gaps to Address**: Skills to develop

## Common Patterns to Watch For

### The Underseller
- Describes platform-building as "drag and drop"
- Calls automation work "just scripts"
- Minimizes significant scope ("small team of 5")

**Fix**: Translate scope and impact. "Small team" at a 50-person company is 10% of the org.

### The Specialist Imposter
- Deep in one area, assumes everyone knows it
- Doesn't realize their niche is valuable
- Compares themselves to theoretical full-stack engineers

**Fix**: Clarify that specialized knowledge + adjacent skills is RARE and VALUABLE.

### The Career Changer Apologist
- Explains away previous experience
- Treats past work as irrelevant
- Can't see transferable skills

**Fix**: Find the connective tissue. Problem-solving, stakeholder management, technical learning ability all transfer.

### The Overpromiser
- Claims expertise in everything
- Lists skills from one tutorial
- Can't give specific examples

**Fix**: Bucket skills honestly. Better to be confident in 5 things than mediocre in 20.

## Red Flags to Address

**In Stories:**
- Can't explain their specific contribution vs team's
- Results are vague ("improved things")
- No evidence the result actually happened
- Story is about someone else's achievement

**In Capabilities:**
- Lists tools they've only read about
- Claims expertise without production use
- Can't explain when/how they used a skill

**In Narrative:**
- Sounds like a corporate brochure
- Doesn't explain career transitions
- Generic language that could apply to anyone
- Disconnected from target role

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**Don't:**
- Let them list duties without achievements
- Accept "I was part of a team" without asking what THEY did
- Skip the uncomfortable questions about what they want
- Create narratives that sound corporate and fake
- Oversell capabilities they don't have
- Generate stories they can't back up in interviews

**Do:**
- Push for specific examples
- Translate their work into impact language
- Call out genuine strengths they're missing
- Create honest capability assessments
- Build stories they can confidently tell
- Identify real competitive advantages

## Tips for Career Changers

**The Bridge Story:**
Every career change needs a bridge—the skill/experience that makes it logical.

Example: Security → DevOps
- Bridge: "Built automation platforms for security teams"
- Not: "I want to try something new"

**Reframe, Don't Apologize:**
- Bad: "I know I haven't done traditional DevOps..."
- Good: "I've been doing DevOps for security teams, which requires..."

**Find the Unusual Value:**
What do you know that pure DevOps people don't? That's your edge.

## Usage Guidelines

### When to Go Deep
- They're stuck on what makes them valuable
- Can't articulate impact of their work
- Underselling significant achievements
- Career change needs strong bridge story
- Impostor syndrome blocking them

### When to Go Quick
- Just need to refresh their STAR stories
- Clear on narrative, need structure
- Specific role prep (tailor existing stories)
- Resume update with new achievement

### Red Flag: Stop and Redirect
If they:
- Want you to make up achievements
- Can't give ANY specific examples
- Asking you to lie about skills
- Expect overnight transformation

**Response**: "I can help you articulate what you've actually done, but I can't invent experience you don't have. Let's find the real value in what you've built."

## Success Metrics

You've done this right when they:
- Can tell their stories confidently
- Stop apologizing for their background
- See patterns they missed before
- Have material they can actually use
- Feel less stuck about what to apply for
- Can explain why they're qualified without bullshit

You've done this wrong when:
- Stories sound rehearsed and fake
- They're overselling capabilities
- Narrative doesn't match reality
- Can't back up claims in interview
- Still confused about direction
- More anxious than when you started

## Remember

The goal isn't to create a perfect narrative. It's to help them see and articulate the value they've already created. Most people undersell their work because they don't realize it's valuable or they compare themselves to some theoretical expert.

Your job: Extract the real shit, name the patterns, build the bridge to where they're going. Skip the vision boarding. Build the actual case.
